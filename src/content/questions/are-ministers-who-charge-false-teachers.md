Not necessarily. Scripture marks false teachers by greed and warns us to watch for it, but many sincere ministers charge only because the practice was handed down to them. We should judge the practice by Scripture and leave hearts to God.

## Proofs
- 2 Peter 2:3
- 1 Corinthians 4:5
- Philippians 1:18
- Romans 14:4
- 2 Timothy 2:25

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Two things are true together.

First, Scripture takes greed in ministry very seriously. False teachers "in their greed... will exploit you with deceptive words" (2 Peter 2:3). Selling ministry is a practice the New Testament associates with false teaching, and the church should not be naive about it.

Second, most Christians who charge for ministry have never been taught otherwise. They inherited the practice from their schools, publishers and mentors. Many give generously, serve sacrificially, and would be grieved to think they were disobeying Christ. To call them wolves would be unjust and unkind.

So we judge practices, not hearts. "Judge nothing before the appointed time; wait until the Lord comes. He will bring to light what is hidden in darkness and will expose the motives of men's hearts" (1 Corinthians 4:5). We can say plainly that charging for ministry is contrary to Christ's command, while treating those who do it as brothers to be persuaded, "in the hope that God may grant them repentance leading to a knowledge of the truth" (2 Timothy 2:25).
