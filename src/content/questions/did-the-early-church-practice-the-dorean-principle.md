Yes. The earliest Christian writings after the New Testament warned that a traveling prophet who asked for money was a false prophet, while teaching that true teachers should be supported by the churches.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8-10
- 3 John 5-8
- 2 John 10-11

The *Didache*, a church manual from around the end of the first century, gave plain instructions about traveling teachers. A visiting apostle was to be welcomed and given bread for the road, but if he asked for money he was to be treated as a false prophet. Anyone who said "in the Spirit," "Give me money," was not to be listened to, unless the money was for others in need. At the same time, the *Didache* told churches to provide for true prophets and teachers who settled among them, giving them firstfruits as was once given to the priests.

That is the dorean principle in practice: support for faithful teachers from the church, and suspicion of any teacher who charges.

Other early writers said the same. *The Shepherd of Hermas* describes the false prophet as one who takes pay for his prophecy. When the Montanist movement of the second century began collecting money and paying its preachers, its critics pointed to that as evidence of its falseness, quoting Jesus' command to take no gold or silver.

These writings are not Scripture, but they show how the first generations after the apostles understood Jesus' words.
