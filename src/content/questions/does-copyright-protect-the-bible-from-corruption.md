No. Copyright was designed to control copying for commercial reasons, not to guard truth. God preserves His Word through many faithful copies, and the church guards sound teaching by teaching, not by legal restrictions.

## Proofs
- Matthew 24:35
- 1 Peter 1:24-25
- 2 Timothy 1:13-14
- Jude 3
- 2 Timothy 4:2-4

Some publishers argue that copyright keeps translations and teaching from being twisted. The argument does not hold up.

- **False teachers make their own.** Cults have always produced their own translations and their own literature. Copyright on a faithful translation did not stop them.
- **Monopolies make mistakes too.** When the King James Version was printed under exclusive royal patents, printers still produced famous misprints. Control over copying did not guarantee accuracy.
- **God preserves His Word through abundance.** The New Testament has come down to us in thousands of manuscripts copied freely by the church. "Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will never pass away" (Matthew 24:35).

The New Testament's way of guarding the truth is pastoral, not legal: "Hold on to the pattern of sound teaching you have heard from me... Guard the treasure entrusted to you, with the help of the Holy Spirit" (2 Timothy 1:13-14). Believers are to "contend earnestly for the faith entrusted once for all to the saints" (Jude 3), and pastors are to "preach the word" and correct error (2 Timothy 4:2).

If someone corrupts a free translation or book, the remedy is to teach the truth and warn against the error, not to lock up the good copy.
