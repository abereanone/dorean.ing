No. The reason Jesus gave, "freely you have received," is as true of every minister as it was of the twelve, and the apostles kept the command long after that mission, offering the gospel free of charge.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Matthew 28:19-20
- 1 Corinthians 9:18
- 2 Corinthians 11:7
- 3 John 7-8

Some instructions in Matthew 10 were clearly for that mission only: go only to the lost sheep of Israel (Matthew 10:5-6), take no bag or staff. Later Jesus changed the provisioning instructions: "the one with a purse should take it, and likewise a bag" (Luke 22:36).

But the command not to charge is different, for three reasons:

1. **Its reason never expires.** "Freely you have received" describes every servant of Christ in every age. No minister has bought what he gives.
2. **The apostles kept it.** Years after the first mission, Paul's reward was still "that in preaching the gospel I may offer it free of charge" (1 Corinthians 9:18), and John's missionaries still went out "accepting nothing from the Gentiles" (3 John 7).
3. **Jesus told the apostles to pass on His commands.** The Great Commission includes "teaching them to obey all that I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:20).

Note also that when Jesus told the disciples to take a purse, He was preparing them for hostility, not telling them to start charging. Carrying their own money is one thing. Selling their message is another.
