No. It governs the ministry of the Word, not ordinary work. Christians should work hard, charge fair prices for their labor, and provide for their families.

## Proofs
- 2 Thessalonians 3:10-12
- Ephesians 4:28
- 1 Timothy 5:8
- Proverbs 31:16-18
- Acts 18:3

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul made tents and sold them (Acts 18:3). Lydia was "a dealer in purple cloth" (Acts 16:14). The noble wife of Proverbs 31 "appraises a field and buys it" and "sees that her gain is good" (Proverbs 31:16-18). Scripture commands work and condemns idleness: "If anyone is unwilling to work, he shall not eat" (2 Thessalonians 3:10).

So a Christian printer may charge for printing, a Christian graphic designer may charge for design, and a Christian bookseller may charge for the paper, ink and shipping of a book. A Christian counselor's secretary is paid a wage. None of that sells ministry.

The line is crossed when the thing being sold is the Word of God itself, or teaching, counsel or worship drawn from it. A carpenter who builds a pulpit may charge for the pulpit. He may not charge the congregation for the sermon preached from it.

When a case is unclear, err on the side of generosity, and ask your elders for counsel.
