The objection is pragmatic, and Christ's command does not bend to pragmatism. In any case people value countless free things, and the most valuable gift of all, the gospel, is offered without price.

## Proofs
- Isaiah 55:1-2
- Matthew 13:44-46
- Psalm 119:72
- Romans 6:23

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

It is often said that counseling works better if the counselee pays, or that people take a free conference less seriously. There are several answers.

1. **Scripture does not give us this option.** Jesus did not say, "Freely give, unless charging would help them value it."
2. **It is not even true in general.** People treasure free things all the time: friendship, family, libraries, the counsel of a wise grandparent.
3. **God's own pattern is the opposite.** The gospel is free, yet it is the most precious thing in the world. Jesus compared the kingdom to treasure for which a man gladly sells everything (Matthew 13:44). It is free, but not cheap. The psalmist says, "The law from Your mouth is more precious to me than thousands of pieces of gold and silver" (Psalm 119:72).
4. **Commitment can be asked for without money.** A counselor can expect homework done and appointments kept. A teacher can ask students to read and attend. Seriousness is shown by obedience, not by payment.

If someone does not value ministry given freely, the remedy is prayer and patience, not a price tag.
