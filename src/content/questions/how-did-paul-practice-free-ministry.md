Paul preached without charging his hearers. He worked with his hands when he had to, accepted gifts from churches that partnered with him, and refused anything that could look like payment from those he was serving.

## Proofs
- 1 Corinthians 9:18
- 2 Corinthians 11:7-9
- 1 Thessalonians 2:9
- Acts 18:3-5
- Acts 20:33-35

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul's pattern shows up in every church he planted:

- **Corinth.** He made tents with Priscilla and Aquila (Acts 18:3), then "devoted himself fully to the word" when Silas and Timothy arrived from Macedonia (Acts 18:5), very likely bringing support. He told the Corinthians he "preached the gospel of God to you free of charge" because "the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied my needs" (2 Corinthians 11:7-9).
- **Thessalonica.** "We worked night and day so that we would not be a burden to anyone while we proclaimed to you the gospel of God" (1 Thessalonians 2:9). Meanwhile the Philippians sent help "again and again" (Philippians 4:16).
- **Ephesus.** "I have not coveted anyone's silver or gold or clothing. You yourselves know that these hands of mine have ministered to my own needs and those of my companions" (Acts 20:33-34).

Paul called this his reward: "That in preaching the gospel I may offer it free of charge" (1 Corinthians 9:18). He did not despise support. He received it gladly from partners. What he would not do was let the people he was evangelizing and teaching pay for what he was giving them.
