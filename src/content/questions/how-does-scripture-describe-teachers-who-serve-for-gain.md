The New Testament repeatedly marks false teachers by their greed: they "regard godliness as a means of gain," exploit people with deceptive words, and teach for dishonorable profit. Faithful elders must be free of the love of money.

## Proofs
- 2 Peter 2:3
- 1 Timothy 6:5
- Titus 1:10-11
- 1 Timothy 3:3
- 1 Peter 5:2
- Jude 11

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Greed is not a side issue in the Bible's portrait of false teachers. It is near the center:

- "In their greed, these false teachers will exploit you with deceptive words" (2 Peter 2:3).
- They "regard godliness as a means of gain" (1 Timothy 6:5).
- "For the sake of dishonorable gain, they undermine entire households and teach things they should not" (Titus 1:11).
- They "rushed for profit into the error of Balaam" (Jude 11).

The positive qualifications for elders say the same thing from the other side. An overseer must be "free of the love of money" (1 Timothy 3:3), and elders must shepherd "not out of greed, but out of eagerness" (1 Peter 5:2).

This does not mean every minister who charges is a false teacher. Many sincere men have inherited the practice without thinking it through. But Scripture gives the church every reason to watch carefully wherever the ministry of the Word has become a source of personal profit, and to arrange its own ministries so that no such suspicion can attach to them.
