By bringing offerings to the Lord through the church, willingly and generously, so that the church can provide for those who labor in the Word. Giving goes to God, and God's house supplies His servants; members do not pay the preacher for the sermon.

## Proofs
- Exodus 35:5
- Numbers 18:21-24
- Deuteronomy 10:8-9
- 2 Corinthians 9:6-7
- 1 Corinthians 16:2

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

When Israel gave for the tabernacle, the command was, "Take from among you an offering to Yahweh. Let everyone whose heart is willing bring an offering to Yahweh" (Exodus 35:5). The offering went to the Lord, not to a particular priest.

God then provided for His servants out of what was given to Him. "I have given to the Levites all the tithes in Israel as an inheritance in return for the work they do" (Numbers 18:21). The tithe was what "the Israelites present to Yahweh as a contribution" (Numbers 18:24). The Levites had no land, because "Yahweh is his inheritance" (Deuteronomy 10:9).

This is a *mediated* obligation. The worshiper brings his offering to God's house; the church, through its appointed officers, distributes it, including to those who preach and shepherd. Compare two scenes:

- A man shakes the preacher's hand at the door and presses fifty dollars into it "for that sermon." That is payment for ministry.
- The same man brings his offering on the Lord's Day to the Lord's house, and the church faithfully supports its pastor. That is worship, and partnership in the gospel.

Scripture's guidance for that giving:

- **Regularly.** "On the first day of every week, each of you should set aside a portion of his income" (1 Corinthians 16:2).
- **Willingly and cheerfully.** "God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7).
- **Generously.** "Whoever sows generously will also reap generously" (2 Corinthians 9:6). The Macedonians gave "according to their ability and even beyond it" (2 Corinthians 8:3).
- **Your best.** God rebuked Israel for bringing Him what they would not offer their governor (Malachi 1:8).
