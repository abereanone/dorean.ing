As God's provision through His people, received with gratitude and held with contentment, never as a price his hearers owe him. He should be free of the love of money and ready to serve whether or not he is paid.

## Proofs
- Philippians 4:11-13
- 1 Timothy 6:6-10
- Hebrews 13:5
- 1 Peter 5:2
- Luke 17:10

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A minister's view of money shapes his ministry. Scripture gives him a clear mindset:

- **Gratitude.** Support comes from God through His people. Paul thanked the Philippians and thanked God for them (Philippians 4:10, 18).
- **Contentment.** "I have learned to be content regardless of my circumstances" (Philippians 4:11). "If we have food and clothing, we will be content with these" (1 Timothy 6:8).
- **Freedom from the love of money.** "Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, for God has said: 'Never will I leave you, never will I forsake you'" (Hebrews 13:5).
- **Eagerness, not greed.** Elders shepherd "not out of greed, but out of eagerness" (1 Peter 5:2).
- **Duty, not merit.** "So you also, when you have done everything commanded of you, should say, 'We are unworthy servants; we have only done our duty'" (Luke 17:10).

A minister who sees his income this way will not think of his sermons, books or counsel as products his hearers owe him for. He will give them away and trust the Lord of the harvest to care for him.
