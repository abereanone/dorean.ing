Ministry should be supported by God's people giving freely, by churches budgeting for it, and sometimes by the minister's own work. Costs are real, but they should be met by support, not by selling the ministry.

## Proofs
- Philippians 4:15-19
- 3 John 8
- 2 Corinthians 9:7-8
- 1 Kings 17:13-16
- Matthew 6:33

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The dorean principle does not pretend that ministry is free to provide. Buildings, salaries, printing, travel and software all cost money. The question is only *who pays*.

- **The church.** Congregations should fund the ministries they believe in, as they fund their pastors.
- **Partners.** Believers and churches who share the vision give as fellow workers (3 John 8; Philippians 4:15-16).
- **The minister's own labor.** Some will work another job and minister freely (Acts 20:34).

Many ministers fear that without fees the work will not be sustainable. But the Lord who sent the twelve with nothing provided for them. When Jesus later asked, "When I sent you out without purse or bag or sandals, did you lack anything?" they answered, "Nothing" (Luke 22:35).

Elijah asked the widow of Zarephath for her last bread, promising that "the jar of flour will not be exhausted and the jug of oil will not run dry" (1 Kings 17:14), and it was so. "God is able to make all grace abound to you, so that in all things, at all times, having all that you need, you will abound in every good work" (2 Corinthians 9:8).
