No. A salary from the church's offerings is the church supporting its servant so that he can give himself to the work. It does not make any sermon, prayer or visit something a member must pay for.

## Proofs
- 1 Timothy 5:17-18
- 1 Corinthians 9:13-14
- Numbers 18:21-24
- Galatians 6:6

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A salary can look like a wage for services, but the arrangement is different in kind from a fee.

- **Who pays:** the congregation as a body, through offerings given to God, not individuals paying for what they personally receive.
- **What it is for:** the pastor's living, so that he can study, preach, shepherd and pray without needing other work. It is not a price attached to particular acts of ministry.
- **Who receives the ministry:** everyone, members and visitors, givers and non-givers alike. Nobody is turned away from the sermon or the hospital visit because they gave little.

This mirrors the Levites. God gave them "all the tithes in Israel as an inheritance in return for the work they do" (Numbers 18:21), but the tithe was presented "to Yahweh as a contribution" (Numbers 18:24). The people gave to God, and God provided for His servants.

A pastor should still think of his salary in this way: not as payment he has earned from customers, but as God's provision through His people, received with gratitude and held with contentment.
