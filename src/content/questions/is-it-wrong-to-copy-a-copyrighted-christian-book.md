No. The dorean principle binds the one who gives ministry, not the one who receives it. Christians must obey the law and not take what an author has withheld, even if they believe he should have given it freely.

## Proofs
- Exodus 20:15
- Romans 13:1
- Romans 13:7
- Matthew 17:24-27
- Philippians 1:18

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

It would be a strange reading of "freely give" to conclude, "therefore I may freely take." Jesus' command is addressed to His servants about what they give. It does not license anyone to break the law or take what belongs to someone else.

Scripture tells us to submit to governing authorities (Romans 13:1) and to "pay everyone what you owe him" (Romans 13:7). When Peter was asked whether Jesus paid the temple tax, Jesus pointed out that the sons of the King should be exempt, and then paid it anyway: "so that we may not offend them" (Matthew 17:27). He bore an unfair burden for the sake of peace.

So if a book of good teaching is under copyright:

- buy it or borrow it lawfully;
- ask the author or publisher whether a free copy or a freely licensed edition is available;
- graciously encourage the author to release his work;
- rejoice that Christ is preached, even imperfectly (Philippians 1:18).

What you should not do is pirate it. Taking it would add a sin of your own to the problem, and it would undercut the witness of those who are asking Christian authors to give their work away.
