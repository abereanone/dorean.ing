In an important sense, yes. A minister is a steward of what belongs to God. Selling what belongs to your master and keeping the money is not commerce but theft, however good the goods are.

## Proofs
- 1 Corinthians 4:1-2
- Psalm 24:1
- Matthew 10:8
- Exodus 20:15

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Men ought to regard us as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God. Now it is required of stewards that they be found faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:1-2).

Picture a clerk at a gas station. A customer wants a pack of gum, so the clerk sells it and puts the money in his own pocket. The gum was real, the customer got what he paid for, and the price was fair. But the clerk stole, because the gum was not his to sell.

The Word of God is God's word. Notice the possessive. No preacher, teacher, counselor or author owns the gospel. He received it, as a steward, to hand out to others on his Master's behalf. When he sells it and keeps the money, he is selling what is not his.

This does not mean every minister who has charged knew he was doing wrong, and it does not make the people who paid guilty. But it shows why the issue matters. It is not only about wisdom or appearances. It is about faithfulness to the Owner. "The earth is Yahweh's, and the fullness thereof" (Psalm 24:1), and His Word most of all.
