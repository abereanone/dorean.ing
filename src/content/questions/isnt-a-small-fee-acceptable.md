The size of the price is not the issue. Any charge makes access to ministry depend on payment, and even a small fee shuts out some people and changes the relationship between minister and hearer.

## Proofs
- James 2:1-4
- Acts 8:20
- Luke 16:10
- Isaiah 55:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A small fee feels harmless, and a cost-recovery fee feels responsible. But consider what any fee does:

- **It is still a condition.** The person who cannot or will not pay is turned away from the teaching or counsel. James warns against arrangements that seat the rich and make the poor stand (James 2:1-4).
- **It still makes the hearer a customer.** The relationship is exchange, whatever the amount.
- **It is still the sale of a gift.** Simon offered money for the gift of God, and Peter did not ask how much (Acts 8:20).

Covering costs is a real need, and the answer is to cover them another way: through the church, through partners, or by separating genuine material costs (food, lodging, printing) from the ministry itself and offering them at cost to those who want them.

"Whoever is faithful with very little will also be faithful with much" (Luke 16:10). A ministry that gives freely when the stakes are small is learning the habits it will need when they are large.
