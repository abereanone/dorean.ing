No. Legalism adds human rules to God's Word or seeks to earn God's favor by obedience. The dorean principle is Christ's own command, obeyed out of love by those already saved by grace. It forbids selling ministry, and it must not be stretched to forbid what Scripture allows, such as supporting ministers.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- John 14:15
- 1 John 5:3
- Mark 7:7-8
- Colossians 2:20-23

Legalism is a real danger. Jesus condemned those who "teach as doctrine the precepts of men" (Mark 7:7), and Paul warned against rules like "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!" that are "based on human commands and teachings" (Colossians 2:21-22).

But obeying a command of Christ is not legalism. "If you love Me, you will keep My commandments" (John 14:15). "His commandments are not burdensome" (1 John 5:3). "Freely give" is Christ's command, not a tradition of men.

The danger runs both ways, though. It would be legalism to go beyond Scripture by:

- forbidding churches to pay their pastors, which Scripture commands;
- condemning every Christian who sells anything at all;
- judging the hearts of ministers who charge, rather than reasoning with them;
- making minute rules for every gray area.

Hold firmly to what Christ commanded, and hold loosely to the details of application. Where a case is unclear, lean toward generosity and seek the counsel of your elders.
