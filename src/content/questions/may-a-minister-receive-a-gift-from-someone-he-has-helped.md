A minister should be careful. A gift that is really payment for ministry received should be declined or redirected, as Elisha refused Naaman. Gifts given as partnership in the work, especially through the church, may be received with thanks.

## Proofs
- 2 Kings 5:15-16
- Philippians 4:17-18
- Matthew 10:8
- 2 Corinthians 12:14

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Not every gift is a fee, but some gifts are fees in disguise. The difference is what the gift means to the giver and what it will mean to everyone watching.

**When to decline.** Naaman wanted to repay Elisha for his healing, and Elisha refused because the gift was a response to the miracle (2 Kings 5:15-16). A counselee who hands the counselor an envelope after every session, or a couple who "tips" the pastor for their wedding, may sincerely want to say thank you, but the gift functions as payment. A minister can gently say, "This was freely given to you. If you want to give, give to the church so that this ministry can serve others too."

**When to receive.** Paul gladly received the Philippians' gifts as "a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, well-pleasing to God," and said he was looking for "the fruit that may be credited to your account" (Philippians 4:17-18). Partnership in the work, given to the Lord, is a blessing to the giver.

Some practical guardrails help: let gifts flow through the church rather than to the minister personally, never suggest or expect a gift, and never treat givers differently from those who give nothing.
