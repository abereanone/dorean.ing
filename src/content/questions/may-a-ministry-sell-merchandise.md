Selling ordinary goods is not selling ministry, but ministries should be careful. Merchandise must not become the price of ministry, and commerce should be kept out of worship and away from the teaching itself.

## Proofs
- John 2:16
- Acts 18:3
- 2 Corinthians 8:21
- Matthew 10:8

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A shirt, a mug or a bake sale is not the ministry of the Word. Paul sold tents (Acts 18:3), and selling ordinary goods to support ministry is not in itself forbidden.

But a few cautions apply:

- **Do not bundle ministry with goods.** "Buy this shirt and receive our teaching series" is selling the teaching. "Give at this level and receive the book" turns a gift into a purchase.
- **Keep commerce out of worship.** Jesus would not let His Father's house become "a marketplace" (John 2:16). Turning the gathered church into a sales floor, with product pitches from the pulpit or merchandise tables at the center of the gathering, works against the free gospel the church preaches.
- **Be transparent.** "We are taking great care to do what is right, not only in the eyes of the Lord but also in the eyes of men" (2 Corinthians 8:21). Supporters should know what goes to the work and what goes to cost.
- **Ask whether giving would be better.** Often a ministry raises money by sales because it is uncomfortable asking for support. But direct, honest giving is the New Testament pattern.
