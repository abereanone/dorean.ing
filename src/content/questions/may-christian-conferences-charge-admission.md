Teaching the Word should not be sold by the ticket. Conferences should be funded by churches and willing givers so that teaching is open to all; necessary costs such as meals can be offered separately and at cost.

## Proofs
- James 2:1-4
- Isaiah 55:1
- Matthew 10:8
- Acts 28:30-31
- 3 John 5-8

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A conference is a gathering for preaching and teaching. Charging admission makes access to that teaching depend on money: those who can pay come in, those who cannot stay out. James warns against exactly this kind of partiality in the church's gatherings: "If you lavish attention on the man in fine clothes and say, 'Here is a seat of honor,' but say to the poor man, 'You must stand'... have you not discriminated among yourselves?" (James 2:3-4).

Paul's practice in Rome is a better model: "welcoming all who came to visit him. Boldly and freely he proclaimed the kingdom of God" (Acts 28:30-31).

Conferences cost real money: venues, travel, sound, food. Those costs can be met without selling the teaching:

- **Host churches budget for it**, as they would for any ministry.
- **Partners give in advance.** Churches and individuals can fund an event so that it is free to attend.
- **Meals and lodging can be separate.** Food and rooms are not ministry, and can be offered at cost to those who want them, while the sessions remain free.
- **Recordings are given away**, so that nobody's access depends on attending.

A conference that would not happen without ticket sales may not need to happen. But many have found that when they make the teaching free, God's people fund it gladly.
