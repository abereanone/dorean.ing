The teaching itself should be given freely, with a free digital copy available to all. Printed copies cost real money, and selling paper at cost (or letting a printer sell it) is not selling the teaching, as long as the content is free.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- 2 Corinthians 2:17
- Proverbs 23:23
- Matthew 5:15-16

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A book of biblical teaching is ministry of the Word in written form. Selling it so that only paying readers may learn from it is selling ministry. Royalties on Christian teaching are a fee for access to the Word as the author has explained it.

But a printed book is also paper, ink, binding and shipping. Those costs are real, and charging for them is not the same as charging for the teaching. The faithful path is to separate the two:

1. **Release the content freely.** Put a free digital edition online where anyone can read it, and dedicate the text to the public domain so that others may print, translate and share it.
2. **Let print copies be sold at cost.** Print-on-demand services and third-party printers can sell physical copies to those who want them, without the author profiting from each sale.
3. **Give copies away where you can.** Many authors and churches give printed books to anyone who asks, funded by supporters.

"Invest in truth and never sell it" (Proverbs 23:23). A lamp is set on a stand so that it "gives light to everyone in the house" (Matthew 5:15). Christian teaching should be as easy to reach as the author can make it.
