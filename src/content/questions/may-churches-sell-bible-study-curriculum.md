Curriculum that teaches Scripture is ministry, so it should be released freely for churches to use, copy and adapt. Ministries that write it should be supported by partners rather than funded by sales and license fees.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Colossians 3:16
- 2 Timothy 2:2
- 3 John 8

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Sunday school lessons, catechism materials, small group guides and youth curricula exist to teach the Bible. When a ministry sells them, or licenses them so that a church must pay again for every copy, every class or every year, the churches it serves are paying for access to biblical teaching.

The effect falls hardest on small and poor congregations, the very churches that most need good material.

A better model:

- write curriculum as a ministry, supported by churches and donors who believe in it;
- publish it freely online, in the public domain, so that any church can print as many copies as it needs and adapt it to its setting;
- sell printed workbooks at cost, if at all, for those who prefer them.

Churches that want good materials can join in by funding their creation, contributing their own lessons, and sharing freely what they have written. "Let the word of Christ richly dwell within you as you teach and admonish one another" (Colossians 3:16) is a command to the whole church, and the whole church can help make the tools for it free.
