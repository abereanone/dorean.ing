Tools whose purpose is to deliver Scripture and biblical teaching (Bible apps, study libraries, prayer and catechism apps) should be offered freely and supported by willing givers. Programming is real work, but the purpose of these tools is ministry.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Isaiah 55:1
- 3 John 8
- 2 Corinthians 9:7

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Software is not ministry in itself. A church's accounting program or a sermon recording tool is ordinary software, and charging for it is ordinary business.

But many Christian apps exist to deliver the Word: Bible readers, reference libraries, catechism and prayer apps, devotional apps. When the content is God's Word or teaching drawn from it, putting it behind a purchase or subscription sells access to ministry. Paid Bible software also often bundles copyrighted translations and commentaries, so that what a believer can study depends on what he can afford.

There are good alternatives already in use:

- **free and open-source Bible software**, developed by volunteers and supporters;
- **public domain translations**, such as the Berean Standard Bible, that any app can include at no cost;
- **donation-supported apps**, free to every user, funded by those who want to see the work continue.

Developers who build these tools for the church deserve support. That support should come as partnership, the same way the church supports its other workers, so that every believer can open the Scriptures without a price tag.
