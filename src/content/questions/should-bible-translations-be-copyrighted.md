No. A translation of Scripture is the Word of God in a people's language, and its use should not depend on a publisher's permission. Translations should be dedicated to the public domain, as the Berean Standard Bible and the World English Bible are.

## Proofs
- Matthew 24:35
- Isaiah 40:8
- 1 Peter 1:24-25
- Matthew 10:8

Most modern English translations are copyrighted. Their usage guidelines typically limit how many verses may be quoted, forbid quoting a whole book, cap how much of a new work may be Scripture, and require a notice crediting the publisher. Printing a translation in full, putting it in an app, or recording it as audio requires a license.

This means God's Word, in the language people actually read, is controlled by publishers. Churches, missionaries and app developers must ask permission and often pay to share Scripture.

There is a better way, and it is growing:

- The **Berean Standard Bible** was dedicated to the public domain in 2023. Anyone may print, quote, record or include it in software without permission. (The verse previews on this site use it.)
- The **World English Bible** has long been in the public domain.
- The **King James Version** is in the public domain in most of the world, although in the United Kingdom it is still under perpetual Crown rights.

The labor of translation is real and deserves support. That support should come from churches and donors, as with any other ministry, so that the finished Word can be given freely: "the word of the Lord stands forever" (1 Peter 1:25), and it should not be locked up for seventy years after the translator's death.
