Yes. Biblical counseling is the ministry of the Word applied to one person's life, so it should be offered without charge. Counselors should be supported by their churches and other believers, not paid by the hour by the people they counsel.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Romans 15:14
- Colossians 3:16
- Galatians 6:1-2
- Galatians 6:6

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Biblical counselors rightly insist that counseling is ministry. Scripture describes believers instructing one another (Romans 15:14), teaching and admonishing one another with the word of Christ (Colossians 3:16), and restoring those caught in sin with gentleness (Galatians 6:1). That is the ministry of the Word in personal conversation.

If counseling is ministry of the Word, then what Christ requires of the ministry of the Word He requires of counseling. We would not ask a pastor to set a price for a sermon or a hospital visit. A session in a counselor's office is the same gospel applied to one person.

This does not mean counselors work for nothing. Their churches should support them as they support pastors, and other believers can join as partners (Galatians 6:6; 3 John 8). Some counselors may choose to work another job and counsel freely in the evenings.

Even the Association of Certified Biblical Counselors, whose work has helped many, says in its standards that the gospel should be offered without price, though it currently leaves the question of fees to each counselor. Many have urged that the standard go further and that counseling be offered freely. A hurting soul should never have to wonder whether help from God's Word depends on what is in the checking account.

See also biblicalcounsel.ing for questions on counseling itself.
