Songs written for the church to sing in worship are ministry, so songwriters should give them freely by dedicating them to the public domain. Their work can be supported by churches and partners instead of license fees.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Colossians 3:16
- Psalm 96:1-2
- Psalm 115:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

A worship song puts the truths of Scripture into the mouths of God's people. When a songwriter writes for the church, he is serving the church in the ministry of the Word.

Copyright turns that service into a license. Every church that wants to project the lyrics, print them, record them or translate them into another language must ask permission and usually pay. The songs that most need to travel, into small churches, poor churches and other languages, are the ones copyright slows down the most.

Songwriters can follow a better path:

- **Dedicate songs to the public domain** with a simple statement (for example, a CC0 dedication), so that any church may sing, print, record and translate them.
- **Sell recordings at cost, or give them away**, while leaving the song itself free.
- **Seek support from churches and partners** who value the ministry of writing for the church.

"Not to us, O Yahweh, not to us, but to Your name be the glory" (Psalm 115:1). A songwriter who gives his songs away loses control over them, but gains a church that can sing them freely in every tongue.
