Yes. Scripture commands churches to provide for those who labor in preaching and teaching. The dorean principle forbids selling ministry, not supporting ministers.

## Proofs
- 1 Timothy 5:17-18
- 1 Corinthians 9:14
- Galatians 6:6
- Luke 10:7

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Some people hear "freely give" and conclude that pastors should never receive money. Scripture says the opposite.

- "Elders who lead effectively are worthy of double honor, especially those who work hard at preaching and teaching" (1 Timothy 5:17).
- "The Lord has prescribed that those who preach the gospel should receive their living from the gospel" (1 Corinthians 9:14).
- "The one who receives instruction in the word must share in all good things with his instructor" (Galatians 6:6).

So a church that can support its pastor should. Refusing to do so is not more spiritual; it disobeys the Lord's own arrangement.

The question is never *whether* ministers may be provided for, but *how*. A church giving its pastor a salary out of its offerings is supporting him. A pastor charging each member for each sermon, prayer or counseling session would be selling. The first is commanded. The second is forbidden.

A pastor may also choose, as Paul sometimes did, to work another job so that he can serve without drawing support. That is a freedom, not a requirement (1 Corinthians 9:6).
