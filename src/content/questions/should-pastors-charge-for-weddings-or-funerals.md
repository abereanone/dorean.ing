No. Officiating a wedding or a funeral is pastoral ministry: preaching, prayer and care for people at the high and low points of life. It should be given freely, though a church may cover real costs such as building use without charging for the ministry itself.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Romans 12:15
- Galatians 6:10
- James 1:27

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

At a wedding the pastor preaches, prays, counsels the couple beforehand, and joins them in the Lord's name. At a funeral he comforts the grieving with the gospel and buries the dead in hope of the resurrection. These are among the most precious moments of pastoral ministry. Scripture calls us to "rejoice with those who rejoice; weep with those who weep" (Romans 12:15).

Setting a fee for that ministry turns it into a service purchased, the same as hiring a photographer or a caterer. It also tells grieving families, who are often outside the church, that the gospel comes with a price list.

Practical guidance:

- **Do not set a fee for officiating, preaching or premarital counseling.** If the pastor is supported by his church, this is part of that ministry.
- **Separate real costs from ministry.** A church may reasonably ask a non-member wedding to cover the cost of cleaning or heating the building, as long as it is presented as a cost of the facility and not the price of the pastor's ministry. Better still, a generous church will often waive it.
- **Redirect gifts.** If a family wants to give, invite them to give to the church or to the poor, so the gift becomes partnership rather than payment.
