Training men in the Scriptures is ministry of the Word, so it should not be sold. Churches and supporters should fund theological training so that it can be offered freely, as many church-based and online seminaries now do.

## Proofs
- 2 Timothy 2:2
- Matthew 10:8
- Acts 19:9-10
- Galatians 6:6

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Paul told Timothy, "the things that you have heard me say among many witnesses, entrust these to faithful men who will be qualified to teach others as well" (2 Timothy 2:2). Training the next generation of teachers is one of the most important ministries in the church. It is still ministry. The Bible, theology and the shepherding of souls are not a product to sell because they are taught at an advanced level.

Many seminaries have served the church well, and those who teach in them should be supported. The question is how. A school that funds its faculty and buildings through churches, donors and endowments can offer instruction freely. A school that makes instruction depend on tuition sells it, and leaves many called men in debt before their ministry begins.

Ways churches and schools are already doing this:

- **church-based training**, where elders train men in the congregation;
- **seminaries funded by partner churches and donors**, tuition-free to students;
- **online seminaries with volunteer faculty**, offering full courses at no cost;
- **separating costs from teaching**, where a student may pay for his own books, housing or an accredited transcript, while the instruction itself is free.

Paul taught "daily discussions in the lecture hall of Tyrannus... so that everyone who lived in the province of Asia... heard the word of the Lord" (Acts 19:9-10). Theological education at its best still looks like that.
