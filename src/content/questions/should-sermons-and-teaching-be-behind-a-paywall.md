No. Putting teaching behind a subscription or paywall restricts the Word to those who pay. Jesus taught openly, and His servants should make their teaching as freely available as they can.

## Proofs
- John 18:20
- Matthew 10:27
- Matthew 10:8
- Acts 20:20

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Jesus said, "I have spoken openly to the world... I said nothing in secret" (John 18:20). He told His disciples, "What I tell you in the dark, speak in the daylight" (Matthew 10:27). Paul "did not shrink back from declaring anything that was helpful to you as I taught you publicly and from house to house" (Acts 20:20).

Paywalls, members-only teaching and premium tiers do the opposite. They take biblical teaching and make it available only to subscribers. Even where the preacher himself is not paid, teaching behind a paywall still conditions access to God's Word on payment.

Ministries that publish online can be funded without paywalls:

- make all teaching public, and ask those who benefit to support the work;
- be honest about needs without promising extra content to those who give;
- avoid "bonus" teaching for donors, which turns gifts back into purchases.

If you come across good teaching that is behind a paywall, do not pirate it. The principle binds the giver; it does not license the receiver to take what is withheld. Pay if you must, ask for a free copy, or encourage the teacher to release it.
