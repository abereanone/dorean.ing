No. Jesus sent the twelve to preach and to heal, and "freely give" covers the whole mission. Paul, who refused payment for preaching, applied the principle to the word, not only to signs.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:7-8
- 1 Corinthians 9:18
- 2 Corinthians 11:7
- 2 Corinthians 2:17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The command sits in a list: "As you go, preach this message: 'The kingdom of heaven is near.' Heal the sick, raise the dead, cleanse the lepers, drive out demons. Freely you have received; freely give" (Matthew 10:7-8). Preaching heads the list. The miracles confirmed the message; they were not a separate business.

Paul's practice settles the question. He did not mention miracles when he described his free ministry. He spoke of preaching: "in preaching the gospel I may offer it free of charge" (1 Corinthians 9:18); "I preached the gospel of God to you free of charge" (2 Corinthians 11:7). And he set himself against those "who peddle the word of God for profit" (2 Corinthians 2:17).

If anything, the word is the greater gift. A healed body will one day die again. The gospel gives eternal life. It would be strange for Jesus to forbid charging for the lesser gift while allowing it for the greater.
