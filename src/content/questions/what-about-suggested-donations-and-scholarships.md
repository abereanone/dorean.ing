These soften a fee but keep it. If giving is presented as payment for the ministry received, or if those who do not give are treated differently, it is still a sale. A free ministry with an honest, separate invitation to support the work is better.

## Proofs
- 2 Corinthians 9:7
- James 2:1-4
- Matthew 10:8
- Matthew 6:3-4

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Many ministries try to avoid turning anyone away while still charging most people. They offer a sliding scale, scholarships for the poor, or a "suggested donation" at the door. The motive is often kind. But each arrangement keeps the logic of a fee:

- **A suggested donation** tied to attending a session or receiving counsel still asks the hearer to pay for what he received.
- **A sliding scale** sets a price, just a different one for each person.
- **Scholarships** require the poor to identify themselves and ask for an exception, which is the partiality James warns against (James 2:1-4).

A better approach:

1. Make the ministry free to everyone, with no expectation attached.
2. Invite support for the ministry as a whole, in a way that is not tied to any particular session, event or product.
3. Treat givers and non-givers exactly the same.
4. Let giving be what Scripture calls it: "what he has decided in his heart to give, not out of regret or compulsion" (2 Corinthians 9:7).
