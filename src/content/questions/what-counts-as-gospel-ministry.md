Gospel ministry is proclaiming and applying God's Word and everything that directly serves that proclamation: preaching, teaching, counseling, prayer, the ordinances, and written or recorded teaching of Scripture.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:7-8
- Acts 6:4
- Colossians 1:28
- 1 Corinthians 2:12-13
- Hebrews 6:1-2

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The command in Matthew 10 covered everything the disciples were sent to do: preaching the kingdom and the works that confirmed it (Matthew 10:7-8). The apostles described their own calling as "prayer and to the ministry of the word" (Acts 6:4), and Paul summarized his work as "admonishing and teaching everyone with all wisdom" (Colossians 1:28).

On that basis, the principle covers:

- preaching and sermons, live or recorded;
- Bible studies, catechism, Sunday school and theological teaching;
- counseling from Scripture;
- prayer for people, weddings and funerals conducted as ministry;
- baptism, the Lord's Supper and church membership;
- books, articles, curricula and courses that teach the Bible;
- songs written for the church to sing in worship;
- Bible translations.

It does not cover ordinary work that Christians do well for the glory of God, such as carpentry, medicine, accounting or making furniture with a verse carved into it. Those are callings, and charging for them is right (2 Thessalonians 3:10-12). The question to ask is: *am I handing on the Word of God and its teaching, or doing ordinary work?*
