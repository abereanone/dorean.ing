Paul argues at length that gospel ministers have a right to be supported, and then explains why he gave up using that right: so that he could offer the gospel free of charge and put no obstacle in its way.

## Proofs
- 1 Corinthians 9:7-14
- 1 Corinthians 9:12
- 1 Corinthians 9:15-18

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

1 Corinthians 9 is often quoted to defend paid ministry. It is better read as Paul's explanation of why he did *not* take payment from the Corinthians.

The first half sets out the right. Paul uses one picture after another: "Who serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat of its fruit? Who tends a flock and does not drink of its milk?" (1 Corinthians 9:7). He cites the law about the ox, the priests who eat from the altar, and the Lord's own command (1 Corinthians 9:9-14). Notice that in each picture the worker is provided for by the owner he serves, not by charging those he serves.

The second half sets the right aside: "But we did not exercise this right. Instead, we put up with anything rather than hinder the gospel of Christ" (1 Corinthians 9:12). "I would rather die than let anyone nullify my boast" (1 Corinthians 9:15). His reward was "that in preaching the gospel I may offer it free of charge" (1 Corinthians 9:18).

The chapter therefore supports two conclusions together: churches should support their ministers, and ministers should never let their hearers' access to the gospel depend on payment.
