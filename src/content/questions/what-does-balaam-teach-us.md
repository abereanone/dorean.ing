Balaam was a prophet who could be hired. He "loved the wages of wickedness," and the New Testament holds him up as the pattern of teachers who turn their gift into a source of profit.

## Proofs
- Numbers 22:7
- Deuteronomy 23:4
- 2 Peter 2:15
- Jude 11
- Revelation 2:14

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The elders of Moab came to Balaam "with the fees for divination in hand" (Numbers 22:7), asking him to curse Israel. God would not let him curse, but Balaam kept looking for a way to earn the reward. Later Israel remembered that Moab "hired Balaam son of Beor... to curse you" (Deuteronomy 23:4).

The apostles return to him again and again:

- False teachers "follow the way of Balaam son of Beor, who loved the wages of wickedness" (2 Peter 2:15).
- They "rushed for profit into the error of Balaam" (Jude 11).
- Christ rebuked the church at Pergamum for holding "the teaching of Balaam" (Revelation 2:14).

Balaam shows how payment works on a man who handles God's words. He did speak the truth when God compelled him. But the reward pulled at him until he found another way to serve the one paying him. A prophet for hire is always tempted to say what his client wants. That is why God's servants are to be supported by His people and not hired by their hearers.
