Copyright lets an author decide who may copy, translate, adapt or share his work, and on what terms. When the work is ministry of the Word, those terms become a price or a condition placed on the people the ministry is for.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- 1 Corinthians 9:18-19
- 2 Timothy 2:9

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Copyright is a legal right granted by the state. In most countries it attaches automatically the moment a work is written down, and it lasts for decades after the author's death. It gives the owner control over copies, translations, recordings and adaptations.

For ordinary creative work, that control is lawful. But when an author or ministry holds copyright over biblical teaching, every reader, church and translator needs permission to use it. That permission often comes with conditions: pay a fee, limit the number of copies, do not translate, do not change anything, credit the owner, do not use it commercially.

Each of those conditions is an obligation the recipient owes the minister in exchange for the ministry. That is the same reciprocity the dorean principle forbids, in legal form.

Paul said, "Though I am free of obligation to anyone, I make myself a slave to everyone, to win as many as possible" (1 Corinthians 9:19). A minister who holds his teaching under copyright reverses that: he stays free, and puts his hearers under obligation to him. "The word of God cannot be chained" (2 Timothy 2:9). It should not be licensed either.
