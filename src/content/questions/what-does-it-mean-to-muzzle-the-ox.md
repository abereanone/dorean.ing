God told Israel not to muzzle an ox while it treads out the grain, so that the animal could eat from the work it was doing. Paul applies it to ministers: a church that leaves its pastor unsupported is muzzling its ox. But feeding the ox is not the same as selling the grain.

## Proofs
- Deuteronomy 25:4
- 1 Corinthians 9:9-11
- 1 Timothy 5:17-18
- Nehemiah 13:10-11

"Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain" (Deuteronomy 25:4). The ox did heavy work, pulling the threshing sledge round and round over the harvest. God commanded that it be free to eat while it worked.

Paul twice applies the law to ministers. "Is it about oxen that God is concerned? Isn't He actually speaking on our behalf? Indeed, this was written for us" (1 Corinthians 9:9-10). And to Timothy: "Elders who lead effectively are worthy of double honor, especially those who work hard at preaching and teaching. For the Scripture says, 'Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain,' and, 'The worker is worthy of his wages'" (1 Timothy 5:17-18).

Two lessons follow:

- **Churches must not muzzle their oxen.** A congregation that does not support the men who labor in the Word is withholding what God says they are owed. When Judah stopped bringing the Levites their portions, "all the Levites and singers responsible for performing the service had gone back to their own fields" (Nehemiah 13:10).
- **The ox eats from the owner's grain, not the buyer's purse.** Notice who feeds the ox: the farmer, from his own harvest. The ox does not sell grain to passers-by. In the same way, God provides for His ministers through His people's offerings, not by letting the ministers sell His Word to those they serve.

So "do not muzzle the ox" and "freely give" do not contradict each other. They describe one arrangement from two sides.
