To peddle is to sell like a merchant hawking goods. Paul contrasted those who sell God's Word for profit with ministers who speak "with sincerity, as men sent from God."

## Proofs
- 2 Corinthians 2:17
- 2 Corinthians 4:2
- 1 Thessalonians 2:3-5

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The verse reads: "For we are not like so many others, who peddle the word of God for profit. On the contrary, in Christ we speak before God with sincerity, as men sent from God" (2 Corinthians 2:17).

The Greek word Paul uses (*kapeleuo*) described a retail trader, a huckster. Some translations soften it to "corrupting" the word, but the picture is commercial: treating the message as merchandise.

Notice what Paul sets against peddling:

- **sincerity**, a motive with nothing hidden in it;
- **being sent from God**, a servant on assignment rather than a merchant with stock;
- **speaking before God**, accountable to Him, not to the market.

It is sometimes said that Paul only condemned those who sold the Word *and* twisted it. But the verse needs no second sin. To sell the Word is already to stand on the wrong side of Paul's contrast. Elsewhere Paul draws the same line: "we never used words of flattery or any pretext for greed" (1 Thessalonians 2:5).
