It says churches must give their pastors all due respect and share their good things with them according to their ability, so that pastors have a comfortable supply, are not entangled in secular affairs, and can show hospitality. It grounds this in the law of nature and the express order of Christ.

## Proofs
- 1 Timothy 5:17-18
- Galatians 6:6
- 2 Timothy 2:4
- 1 Timothy 3:2
- 1 Corinthians 9:6-14

The Second London Baptist Confession of 1689, chapter 26, paragraph 10, reads:

> The work of pastors being constantly to attend the service of Christ, in his churches, in the ministry of the word and prayer, with watching for their souls, as they that must give an account to him; it is incumbent on the churches to whom they minister, not only to give them all due respect, but also to communicate to them of all their good things according to their ability, so as they may have a comfortable supply, without being themselves entangled in secular affairs; and may also be capable of exercising hospitality towards others; and this is required by the law of nature, and by the express order of our Lord Jesus, who hath ordained that they that preach the Gospel should live of the Gospel.

Several things stand out:

- **The duty belongs to the church.** It is "incumbent on the churches to whom they minister." The confession says nothing about pastors charging those they serve.
- **The goal is freedom for the work.** A "comfortable supply" keeps a pastor from being "entangled in secular affairs," as a soldier "refrains from entangling himself in civilian affairs" (2 Timothy 2:4), so that he can attend constantly to the Word, prayer and souls.
- **Support makes hospitality possible.** Elders must be hospitable (1 Timothy 3:2). A starved pastor cannot open his table to others.
- **Even nature teaches it.** The confession says support is required "by the law of nature" as well as by Christ's command (1 Corinthians 9:14).

This is exactly the dorean principle: the church supports its ministers so that they can give the ministry freely.
