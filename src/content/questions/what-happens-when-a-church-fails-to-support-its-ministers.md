Ministers are pulled away from the work into secular labor, the house of God is neglected, and the pressure to sell ministry grows. Scripture calls withholding what belongs to God a form of robbing Him.

## Proofs
- Nehemiah 13:10-11
- Malachi 3:8-10
- 1 Timothy 5:18
- 2 Timothy 2:4

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

In Nehemiah's day the people stopped bringing the Levites' portions. The result: "all the Levites and singers responsible for performing the service had gone back to their own fields." Nehemiah rebuked the officials: "Why has the house of God been neglected?" (Nehemiah 13:10-11). Unsupported ministers had to feed their families some other way, and God's house suffered.

The same thing happens now. A pastor who must work full-time elsewhere has little left for study, prayer, visiting the sick, counseling and preparing to preach. And a ministry that is not supported faces a constant temptation to fund itself by selling: charging for counseling, tickets, books, licenses.

Malachi puts it sharply: "Will a man rob God? Yet you are robbing Me! But you ask, 'How do we rob You?' In tithes and offerings" (Malachi 3:8). When God's people keep back what belongs to Him, they muzzle the ox that treads out their grain.

So the dorean principle cuts both ways. It tells ministers not to sell what is God's. It also tells congregations to give faithfully, so that their ministers never need to. A church that wants its pastor to give the Word freely should make sure he is freely supported.
