Colabor is support given to God's servants as a partnership in His work: believers give to the Lord, out of obligation to Him, so that His servants can keep serving. Scripture encourages it.

## Proofs
- 3 John 5-8
- Philippians 4:14-18
- Philippians 1:5
- Galatians 6:6
- 1 Corinthians 9:7-11

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

John praised Gaius for helping traveling preachers who "went out on behalf of the Name, accepting nothing from the Gentiles." Then he drew the conclusion: "we ought to support such men, so that we may be fellow workers for the truth" (3 John 7-8). Supporting them makes the giver a fellow worker. That is colabor.

Several things mark it out:

- **It is owed to God, not bought from the minister.** Paul's illustrations in 1 Corinthians 9 (the soldier, the vineyard, the flock, the ox) all picture a worker provided for by the one he serves under, not by the people he serves.
- **It shares the work.** The Philippians' "partnership in the gospel" (Philippians 1:5) included their gifts. They were part of Paul's mission, not his customers.
- **It does not buy anything.** The gift does not purchase a sermon, a session, or access. Those are given freely to everyone, including people who give nothing.

Giving to your church so that your pastor can study, preach and shepherd is colabor. So is supporting a missionary, or a counselor your church has set apart, or a writer whose work is given away.
