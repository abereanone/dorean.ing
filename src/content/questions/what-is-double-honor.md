Double honor includes both respect and material support. Paul ties it to the law of the ox and the saying that the worker is worthy of his wages, so it plainly includes provision from the church.

## Proofs
- 1 Timothy 5:17-18
- 1 Thessalonians 5:12-13
- Hebrews 13:17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

"Elders who lead effectively are worthy of double honor, especially those who work hard at preaching and teaching. For the Scripture says, 'Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain,' and, 'The worker is worthy of his wages'" (1 Timothy 5:17-18).

The reasons Paul gives (the ox and the worker's wages) show that "honor" here includes material provision, just as honoring widows a few verses earlier included supporting them (1 Timothy 5:3). The church is also told, "In love, hold them in highest regard because of their work" (1 Thessalonians 5:13).

This honor is owed by the church to its elders. It is part of the church's obedience to Christ. It is not a fee the elders charge for each act of shepherding. An elder who preaches, counsels, visits the sick and prays with the dying gives all of that freely, and the church freely honors him for it.
