Reciprocity is payment made to a minister in exchange for ministry, so that receiving the ministry depends on paying for it. It turns a gift into a transaction, which Christ forbade and Paul refused.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- 2 Corinthians 2:17
- 2 Corinthians 12:14
- 2 Kings 5:15-16
- Micah 3:11

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

In ordinary life reciprocity is fine. You pay the plumber, and the plumber fixes the sink. Ministry of the Word is different, because the minister is handing out what belongs to God.

Reciprocity in ministry looks like:

- a fee charged for a counseling session;
- an admission price for teaching;
- a paywall in front of sermons or Bible lessons;
- a license fee charged to churches for using a worship song or a Bible translation;
- royalties as the condition for reading biblical teaching.

Scripture treats this seriously. Naaman tried to repay Elisha for his healing, and Elisha refused (2 Kings 5:15-16). Micah condemned priests who "teach for a price" (Micah 3:11). Paul would not let the Corinthians pay him, because "I am not seeking your possessions, but you" (2 Corinthians 12:14), and he set himself apart from those "who peddle the word of God for profit" (2 Corinthians 2:17).

The problem is not that money is evil, or that ministers must be poor. The problem is that a price changes the relationship. The hearer becomes a customer, and the minister becomes a vendor of God's gift.
