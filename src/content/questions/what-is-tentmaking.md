Tentmaking is supporting yourself with ordinary work while you minister, as Paul did by making tents. It is an honorable choice that keeps ministry free, but Scripture does not require it of every minister.

## Proofs
- Acts 18:3
- Acts 20:34-35
- 1 Thessalonians 2:9
- 1 Corinthians 9:6
- 2 Thessalonians 3:8-9

Paul worked as a tentmaker in Corinth (Acts 18:3) and reminded the Ephesian elders, "these hands of mine have ministered to my own needs and those of my companions" (Acts 20:34). In Thessalonica, "we worked night and day so that we would not be a burden to anyone while we proclaimed to you the gospel of God" (1 Thessalonians 2:9).

He gave two reasons: so that his hearers would not be burdened, and to give an example of hard work: "Not that we lack this right, but we wanted to offer ourselves as an example for you to imitate" (2 Thessalonians 3:9).

But Paul also asked, "Or are Barnabas and I the only apostles who must work for a living?" (1 Corinthians 9:6). The other apostles did not, and Paul did not blame them. When support came from Macedonia, Paul set his tents aside and "devoted himself fully to the word" (Acts 18:5).

So tentmaking is one faithful way to keep ministry free. Church support is another. What is not faithful is the third way: charging the people you minister to.
