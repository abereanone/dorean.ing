Support is given to God for His servant so that the work can go on; a sale is a price paid to the minister in exchange for the ministry. Support flows from obligation to God; a sale creates a debt to the minister.

## Proofs
- 1 Corinthians 9:7-14
- Philippians 4:15-18
- 3 John 5-8
- 2 Corinthians 11:7-9

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Think of the difference between a missionary supported by churches and a speaker who charges admission.

- **Support** comes from God's people because God has commanded His servants to be cared for. The giver gives to the Lord, as a partner in the work, and the minister receives it from the Lord. Paul called the Philippians' gifts "a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, well-pleasing to God" (Philippians 4:18). They "partnered with me in the matter of giving and receiving" (Philippians 4:15).
- **A sale** is an exchange between the minister and the person he serves. The hearer pays the speaker, the counselee pays the counselor, the reader pays for permission to read. Access depends on payment, and the obligation runs straight from the one who receives ministry to the one who gives it.

Paul accepted support from Macedonia while preaching in Corinth, and he refused to take anything from the Corinthians themselves (2 Corinthians 11:7-9). Same apostle, same kind of money, opposite answers, because one was partnership in the gospel and the other would have looked like payment for it.

Writers on this subject call the first **colabor** (working together with God's servants) and the second **reciprocity** (paying back the one who served you).
