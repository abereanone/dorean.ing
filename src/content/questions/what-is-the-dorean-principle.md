The dorean principle is Jesus' command that the ministry of His Word be given freely: those who serve in the gospel may be supported by God's people, but they must never sell the ministry itself.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- 1 Corinthians 9:14
- 1 Corinthians 9:18
- 2 Corinthians 2:17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

The name comes from the Greek word *dorean*, "freely" or "without cost," in Jesus' command to the twelve: "Freely you have received; freely give" (Matthew 10:8).

The principle holds two truths together that are often pulled apart:

- **Ministers may be supported.** "The Lord has prescribed that those who preach the gospel should receive their living from the gospel" (1 Corinthians 9:14).
- **Ministry must not be sold.** Paul's reward was "that in preaching the gospel I may offer it free of charge" (1 Corinthians 9:18), and he refused to be like those "who peddle the word of God for profit" (2 Corinthians 2:17).

The difference is not the amount of money, or even whether money changes hands. The difference is *what the money is for*. A gift given to God's servant so that the work can go on is support. A price paid in exchange for preaching, teaching, counseling, prayer or a Bible lesson is a sale.

So the principle can be put in one sentence: **ministry should be supported, not sold.**
