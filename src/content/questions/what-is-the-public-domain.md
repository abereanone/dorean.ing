Works in the public domain belong to no one, so anyone may copy, print, translate, record and share them without asking. Dedicating ministry work to the public domain is the clearest way to give it freely.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- Acts 4:32
- 1 Corinthians 4:7
- 2 Timothy 2:2

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

When copyright expires, or when an author waives it, a work enters the public domain. No one owns it. The great hymns of the past, the works of the Reformers and the Puritans, and the old Bible translations are in the public domain, which is why they can be printed and shared so widely.

An author does not have to wait seventy years after his death. He can dedicate his work to the public domain now. The most common tool is the **Creative Commons Zero (CC0)** dedication, a short legal statement in which the author waives all his rights to the extent the law allows.

For ministry, this matters because:

- **no one needs permission** to print, quote or translate the work;
- **churches and missionaries can adapt it** to their own settings and languages;
- **the work outlives the author's control**, so it cannot later be withdrawn or put behind a paywall;
- **it matches the gospel**, which was given to us without conditions.

"What do you have that you did not receive?" (1 Corinthians 4:7). Everything a minister knows of God's Word he received. Dedicating his work to the public domain returns it to the church as freely as it came to him.
