Gehazi took payment for a healing that God had given freely through Elisha. Elisha had refused Naaman's gift, and Gehazi was struck with Naaman's leprosy for turning God's mercy into a transaction.

## Proofs
- 2 Kings 5:15-16
- 2 Kings 5:20-27

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Naaman, the Syrian commander, was healed of leprosy after dipping in the Jordan as Elisha told him. He came back with a large gift, and Elisha answered, "As surely as Yahweh lives, before whom I stand, I will not accept it" (2 Kings 5:16). Naaman urged him, and he still refused.

Elisha's refusal was a message to a pagan general: the God of Israel is not a healer for hire. His mercy is not bought.

Gehazi saw it differently: "my master has spared this Aramean, Naaman, by not accepting what he brought. As surely as Yahweh lives, I will run after him and get something from him" (2 Kings 5:20). He chased Naaman down, invented a story, took silver and clothing, and hid it.

Elisha's rebuke is striking: "Is this the time to accept money and clothing...?" (2 Kings 5:26). The judgment matched the sin. The disease God had freely removed from Naaman clung to the man who sold the gift (2 Kings 5:27).

Gehazi stands in Scripture as a warning to every servant of God who would profit from what God gives freely through his hands.
