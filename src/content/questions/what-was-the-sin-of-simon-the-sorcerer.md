Simon thought the gift of God could be bought with money. Peter condemned him and called him to repent, because spiritual gifts and ministry are not commodities.

## Proofs
- Acts 8:18-24
- Matthew 10:8
- Isaiah 55:1

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

When Simon saw that the Holy Spirit was given through the apostles' hands, "he offered them money. 'Give me this power as well,' he said" (Acts 8:18-19). Peter's answer was severe: "May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could buy the gift of God with money!" (Acts 8:20). He told Simon, "You have no part or share in our ministry, because your heart is not right before God" (Acts 8:21), and called him to repent.

Simon's name gave the church the word *simony*: buying or selling spiritual things. For centuries the church condemned simony in both directions, the purchase of church office and the sale of spiritual ministry, because both treat God's gifts as merchandise.

The lesson reaches past Simon. If the gift of God cannot be bought, it cannot rightly be sold either. Whoever puts a price on ministry of the Word is making Simon's mistake from the other side of the table.
