When Jesus sent out the twelve to preach the kingdom, He told them, "Freely you have received; freely give," and sent them without money, trusting God to provide for them through those who received them.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:7-10
- Luke 10:7
- Luke 9:1-3

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

In Matthew 10 Jesus sends the twelve out to preach that "the kingdom of heaven is near" (Matthew 10:7), to heal and to cast out demons. Then He gives them two instructions that belong together.

First, they are not to charge: "Freely you have received; freely give" (Matthew 10:8).

Second, they are not to provide for themselves in advance: "Do not take along any gold or silver or copper in your belts... for the worker is worthy of his provisions" (Matthew 10:9-10).

So the disciples were to receive their food and lodging, but from the households that welcomed them as messengers of the Lord, not as a fee from the people they healed or taught. Luke records the same mission to the seventy-two with the same reasoning: "For the worker is worthy of his wages" (Luke 10:7).

The command and the provision come in the same breath. Jesus did not ask His servants to choose between giving freely and being cared for. He commanded them to give freely and promised they would be cared for.
