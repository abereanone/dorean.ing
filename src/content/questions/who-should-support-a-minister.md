God's people, giving through the church and as partners in the work, out of obedience to the Lord. Support should not come as a charge on the person receiving the ministry.

## Proofs
- Galatians 6:6
- Philippians 4:15-16
- 3 John 5-8
- 1 Corinthians 16:2
- 2 Corinthians 9:7

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

In the New Testament, provision for ministers comes from:

- **The local church** that the minister serves, through its regular giving. Paul told the Corinthians to set money aside "on the first day of every week" (1 Corinthians 16:2), and Galatians 6:6 tells those taught in the word to share with their teacher.
- **Partner churches** that send and supply ministers working elsewhere, as the Philippians did for Paul (Philippians 4:15-16).
- **Individual believers** who help traveling servants of the gospel, as Gaius did, "so that we may be fellow workers for the truth" (3 John 8).

All of this is giving. "Each one should give what he has decided in his heart to give, not out of regret or compulsion. For God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7).

What is missing from the New Testament is the minister collecting a set price from the people he is preaching to, teaching or counseling. Support comes *around* the ministry, from the body of Christ, so that the ministry itself can be given away.
