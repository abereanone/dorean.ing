Because they had turned the place of worship into a market. Jesus would not let God's house become a place of commerce, and His zeal shows how seriously God takes trade in holy things.

## Proofs
- John 2:13-17
- Matthew 21:12-13
- Mark 11:15-17

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

Twice Jesus cleared the temple courts. At the first, "He made a whip out of cords and drove all from the temple courts," overturned the money changers' tables, and said, "Get these out of here! How dare you turn My Father's house into a marketplace!" (John 2:15-16). His disciples remembered the psalm: "Zeal for Your house will consume Me" (John 2:17).

At the second, near the end of His ministry, He "drove out all who were buying and selling there" and said, "My house will be called a house of prayer. But you are making it a den of robbers" (Matthew 21:12-13).

Some say Jesus objected only to dishonest prices. But in John's account He charges them with making the house a *marketplace*, and in Matthew He drove out buyers as well as sellers. The commerce itself was the offense in that place.

Christians today are God's house, and the gathered church is His temple (1 Corinthians 3:16; Ephesians 2:21-22). The lesson is not that every sale near a church building is sin. It is that the worship and ministry of God must never be turned into trade.
