Because the Philippians gave as partners in his mission to others, while money from the Corinthians, among whom he was then preaching, would have been payment by his hearers for the gospel he was bringing them.

## Proofs
- 2 Corinthians 11:7-9
- 2 Corinthians 12:13-14
- Philippians 4:15-18
- Philippians 1:5

<!-- LONG_ANSWER -->

This question is the key that unlocks Paul's practice. The Philippians were poor (2 Corinthians 8:1-2), and the Corinthians were comparatively well off. If Paul's concern were simply not to impose a hardship, he would have taken money from Corinth and spared Philippi. He did the reverse.

The difference was the *relationship* the money expressed:

- The Philippians "partnered with me in the matter of giving and receiving" (Philippians 4:15). Their gifts sent Paul out to others. Paul called them "a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, well-pleasing to God" (Philippians 4:18). They were giving to God.
- In Corinth, Paul was the one serving them. Money from them would have been an exchange: they receive the gospel, Paul receives their money. So he said, "I will not be a burden, because I am not seeking your possessions, but you" (2 Corinthians 12:14).

Paul even said he "robbed other churches by accepting their support in order to serve you" (2 Corinthians 11:8). He would rather lean on other churches than let his hearers pay for his preaching.
