Most Creative Commons licenses still attach conditions: credit the author, do not change it, do not sell it, or share it on the same terms. Those conditions still place obligations on recipients. Only a public domain dedication such as CC0 gives the work away without conditions.

## Proofs
- Matthew 10:8
- John 7:18
- Philippians 2:3-4
- 1 Corinthians 3:6-7

Creative Commons licenses are a step toward openness, and many ministries use them with good intentions. But each license condition is a requirement on the person receiving the work:

- **Attribution (BY):** you must credit the author. That makes the author's name a condition of using the teaching.
- **NoDerivatives (ND):** you may not translate, adapt or revise. That blocks translation into other languages and adaptation for other settings.
- **NonCommercial (NC):** you may not use it in anything sold. That stops a printer selling copies at cost and creates confusion about churches and ministries.
- **ShareAlike (SA):** anything you make with it must carry the same license, which brings all the other conditions along with it.

Each one is a small piece of reciprocity: the minister gives, and in return the recipient must do something for the minister.

It is right to want credit given honestly, and taking credit for someone else's work is a sin of its own (false witness). But "he who seeks the glory of the One who sent Him is a man of truth" (John 7:18). Ministers do not need legal control over their names to serve faithfully. A CC0 dedication lets the work go wherever God sends it.
